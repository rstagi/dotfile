# Research, evidence, analogy — the subroutines

The mechanics behind Steps 4 and 6 of the skill. Short attributions; full bibliography in
[`../../talk/references/sources.md`](../../talk/references/sources.md). Prefer **Perplexity MCP tools**
when loaded (`perplexity_search`, `perplexity_research`, `perplexity_ask` — they return citations);
**degrade to `WebSearch` / `WebFetch`** otherwise.

---

## Evidence: SIFT + CRAAP, traced to the primary source

Every number on a stage is a trust contract. The audience can't check your sources live, so you do it
for them — and you state the uncertainty they can't see.

**SIFT — the four moves** (Mike Caulfield):
1. **Stop.** Before quoting, notice you don't yet know who's behind this claim.
2. **Investigate the source.** Read *laterally* — open new tabs about the source, not the source's own
   "About" page. Who funded the study? Is the outlet credible?
3. **Find better/original coverage.** A blog quoting a press release quoting a study is three hops
   from truth. Find the study.
4. **Trace to the original** context. Quotes get clipped, charts get truncated, "up to" hides the
   median. Read the primary.

**CRAAP — the source checklist** (Blakeslee, CSU Chico): **C**urrency (is it current enough to still
hold?), **R**elevance (does it actually address your claim?), **A**uthority (who, and what standing?),
**A**ccuracy (is it verifiable, peer-reviewed, replicated?), **P**urpose (selling, advocating, or
informing?).

**Record in the evidence list:** the claim, the **primary-source URL** (not the secondary write-up),
and the **uncertainty** — sample size, "reported but not independently replicated", "projection not
observation", "self-reported". State it on stage too; honest uncertainty reads human and builds more
trust than false confidence.

**Never manufacture precision.** "Improves retention by 47%" with no source is a slop tell, not
evidence. If you can only support "meaningfully better in a controlled study", say exactly that.

## Human-scaling numbers (Knaflic / Rosling)

Big numbers don't land; felt comparisons do. (Knaflic, *Storytelling with Data*; Rosling, Gapminder.)

- Reduce to a per-person or per-room scale: "that's one for every person in this room."
- Anchor to a known object: a unit of time, money, distance the audience already feels.
- Compare to a baseline, don't state in a vacuum: "3× the previous record" beats "1.4 million".
- One number per beat. A wall of statistics is a flatline; pick the one that carries the throughline
  and make *it* physical.

## The analogy subroutine

Run this for each thing the audience finds abstract. The goal is an analogy that maps a **deep
relation**, sits between too-obvious and too-distant, and never collides with a second metaphor family.

1. **Abstract to the core relation.** Strip surface features; write the bare relation — *what depends
   on what, what causes what*. (e.g., not "a cache" but "a small fast store that fronts a big slow one
   and goes stale".)
2. **Search distant domains** for the same relation. Force combinations (James Webb Young: an idea is
   a new combination of old elements). Tools:
   - **Koestler bisociation** — collide two unrelated frames; the spark is the analogy.
   - **SCAMPER** — Substitute / Combine / Adapt / Modify / Put-to-other-use / Eliminate / Reverse the
     concept and see what domain each move lands you in.
   - **Biomimicry** — does nature solve this relation? (immune systems, foraging, erosion, symbiosis).
3. **Validate** with two tests:
   - **Gentner structural alignment** — do the *relations* line up one-to-one, or just the surface
     decorations? "Atom is like a solar system" works because the orbital relation maps; it fails if
     you start matching the sun's color. Map relations, not features.
   - **Boundary test** — where does it break? Find the limit yourself and *name it on stage* before a
     skeptic in row three does. "The analogy holds for X; it stops at Y" is a credibility move.
4. **One family only.** Don't open with a "journey" metaphor and close with a "war" metaphor. Pick the
   deepest-mapping family and live inside it for the whole talk.

Worked archetypes (sources.md): Feynman explaining heat with ice water; Rosling using a washing
machine to carry the whole industrialization argument. Avoid metaphors that carry unwanted baggage
("war on cancer" militarizes; "spreading like a virus" for something good).

## Idea generation — James Webb Young's five steps

When you're stuck for the hook, the moment, or the analogy, run the classic process
(*A Technique for Producing Ideas*):

1. **Gather material** — specific (this talk, this audience) and general (wide reading).
2. **Digest it** — turn it over, look for relationships, make rough partial combinations.
3. **Drop it** — hand it to your subconscious; do something unrelated.
4. **The idea arrives** — usually when you're not trying (shower, walk).
5. **Shape and develop it** — submit the raw idea to daylight and the keep-it-human gates.

Pair with bisociation / SCAMPER / biomimicry (above) to push the "general material" into distant
domains where the non-obvious connections live.

## Hook menu (Step 6) — open with a curiosity gap, never your bio or the agenda

Pick one, tie it directly to the throughline, land it in ~30–60 seconds, and **write the opening line
by hand** (keep-it-human Gate E):

- **Provocative question** — one the audience can't answer yet but now wants to.
- **Startling traced statistic** — must clear SIFT/CRAAP; the surprise comes from a real number.
- **Story in medias res** — drop into the middle of a moment (Jill Bolte Taylor's stroke open).
- **Bold claim / contrarian thesis** — state the shift outright, then spend the talk earning it.
- **Physical demo / prop** — Bill Gates's **2009** TED mosquito jar (sources.md — cite the 2009 talk,
  not the 2015 one). The prop *is* the curiosity gap.

Avoid: "Hi, I'm…, and today I'm going to talk about…" — bios and agendas open nothing.

---

## Close menu (Step 6) — callback + the moment + ONE CTA

- **Callback** to the hook's image/question — closes the loop the open opened.
- **The memorable moment** lands here if you saved it (or is reinforced if it landed mid-talk).
- **One specific, scoped CTA** — one action, doable Monday, not "go transform your industry". Scope it
  small enough that someone actually does it.
- **A closing beat only this speaker could write** — an unresolved question, an admission you were
  wrong, a conviction. Write it by hand. (Highest-signal human marker — keep-it-human Gate B.)

Avoid the **bookended self-summary** (closer restates the opener with no payoff) and "in conclusion".
